Current and future classification,
on one screen

Every corridor in the ROCOG planning area, scored against the principles a classification update has to apply — and shown with what its designation already commits the County to on the ground.

What this is. A working example built on Olmsted County's published GIS. It is not deployed software, not a ROCOG product, and it recommends no classification change. The criteria and weights below are ChiResearch's own proposal, offered to be argued with — not adopted policy. What it does is rank where a planner should look, and show the evidence behind each position.

Two systems, one view

The federal functional class governs funding and design today. ROCOG's designation records the intended future function. They are maintained as separate maps. Here each corridor carries both.

What a classification commits

A designation is a land commitment. The County preserves right-of-way by class, and Access Management Ordinance Ch. 1300 sets driveway and street spacing by class. Both are shown per corridor.

Weights you can move

The ranking is a weighted sum of six criteria. Move the weights and it recomputes against all 1,835 corridors in the browser. Disagreement with the method should be testable, not theoretical.

Method, and what it cannot tell you

How a corridor is scored

    A corridor is a named route within one jurisdiction. Corridors under 0.10 miles are dropped. Interchange ramps are excluded — they inherit the mainline designation while carrying a Local federal class, and counting them as corridors inflates everything.

    Limits worth stating

    1. The hierarchy correspondence is ours. ROCOG has nine designations, the federal system six, and no published crosswalk between them. Two defensible mappings are run; the table shows how far each corridor moves between them.
    2. Right-of-way coverage is matched by name within jurisdiction. It detects a missing record, not an inadequate width.
    3. 41.2 miles sit outside the land use overlay, and the planning area extends past the county line into Pine Island and Chatfield, which this county data does not reach.
    4. Centerline miles, not travel. No volume, crash, freight or transit data. This measures how the network is labelled, not how it performs.
    5. One figure excluded. Every segment carries a federal class, so "coverage" is 100% by construction. Nothing is inferred from it.
